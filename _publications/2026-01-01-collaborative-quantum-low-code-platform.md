abstract: |
  Quantum computing remains challenging for beginners and software practitioners due to its unfamiliar computational model and the need for specialized expertise in designing and testing quantum applications. In addition, developing such applications requires deep knowledge of quantum SDKs, which further increases the barrier to adoption. To address these challenges, low-code platforms offer a promising direction by enabling users to build applications through high-level abstractions rather than manual programming. However, existing quantum low-code platforms provide limited support for collaboration across users with different expertise levels.

  In this paper, we present an extended version of Qonstruct that introduces synchronous real-time collaboration, element-level locking, version history, collaborative validation, and experience-aware interfaces that adapt terminology and abstraction levels to users’ expertise. We further demonstrate how these capabilities enable beginners and quantum experts to collaboratively build and execute Grover's search algorithm using a shared low-code model. Our evaluation shows that experience-aware representations improve beginners' understanding of quantum concepts, while mixed-expertise collaboration enables beginners to achieve 100% modeling correctness, matching the expert group.
